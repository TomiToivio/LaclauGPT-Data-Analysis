## Theoretical definitions to apply

- **Articulation** — a practice establishing relations among elements that modifies their identities. The analytical object is the relation, not the isolated term.
- **Nodal point** — a signifier that partially fixes the relations among other signifiers *within this discourse*. Requires evidence that it organises relations, not merely prominence.
- **Floating signifier** — a signifier contested between rival projects, with evidence of *competing contextual fixations*. Normally a corpus-level candidate.
- **Tendentially empty signifier** — a signifier representing a wider equivalential chain or an absent fullness. Requires evidence of representational expansion over a heterogeneous chain. Ambiguity, breadth or slogan status is **not** sufficient.
- **Demand** — an articulated request/claim. Distinguish an actual demand of a subject from a described, reported or hypothetical one.
- **Collective subject (Us / the people / we)** — requires evidence of constructed membership, shared demands and representation, not mere mention of a group.
- **Chain of equivalence** — heterogeneous demands/identities linked by a shared relation to an obstacle. Requires evidence of the linkage.
- **Chain of difference** — relations that maintain distinction among demands or positions.
- **Antagonistic frontier (Them / the Other)** — a force represented as blocking or threatening the identity/project. Criticism, disagreement or negative sentiment is **not** sufficient.
- **Affective investment** — affects attached to identities, demands and objects. Sentiment polarity is **not** affective investment.
- **Sociotechnical imaginary** — a collectively held, institutionally stabilised vision of a desirable social order enabled by technology. A prediction, fear, aspiration or scenario is **only** a document-level candidate; establishing an imaginary requires comparison across actors/institutions.
