# AI26 project background v1

AI26 studies ideological contestation over artificial intelligence across multiple public arenas. This project context is a compact analytical prior, not evidence about any current source item.

The study may compare elite, grassroots, research, policy and parliamentary discourse. AI is treated as a contested sociotechnical and political object whose meaning is articulated through competing claims about capability, risk, ownership, governance, labour, democracy, progress and desirable or feared futures.

Sensitising formations may include accelerationist or techno-optimist positions, existential-risk or AI-safety discourse, Critical AI positions, anti-AI or restrictive positions, and left techno-optimist or democratic-control positions. These categories are provisional starting points. Do not assign an actor or document to a formation merely because the actor is commonly associated with it, and allow contradiction, hybridity and abstention.

For source-level interpretation, distinguish what the current source actually says from project background, researcher codebooks, retrieved prior material and periodic summaries. Claims about floating or empty signifiers, hegemony, stable formations, sociotechnical imaginaries and temporal shifts normally require corpus-level comparison beyond one document.
