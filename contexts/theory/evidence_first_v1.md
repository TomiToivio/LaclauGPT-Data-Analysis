# Evidence-first discourse analysis context v1

Use these distinctions only for theory-guided interpretive stages. This resource is methodological context, not evidence from the current source item.

- Articulation is relational: identify what elements are linked and how. Co-occurrence or frequency alone is insufficient.
- A nodal point is a structuring function in a discourse, not simply a frequent word.
- A floating signifier requires competing attempts at fixation across discursive formations. Ambiguity or polysemy alone is insufficient.
- An empty-signifier candidate must plausibly represent a broader equivalential chain or projected social fullness. Do not infer emptiness from vagueness alone.
- Equivalence and difference are relational logics. Preserve the relation and evidence rather than reducing them to labels.
- Antagonism or a political frontier requires construction of an obstacle/opponent and a boundary of identity. Negative sentiment or disagreement alone is insufficient.
- Collective subjects must be discursively constructed in the source evidence.
- Affective investment is not equivalent to sentiment classification.
- Formation labels are provisional analytical constructions and sensitising categories, not fixed identities of actors or sources.
- Document-level candidates may require corpus-level comparison and human validation.
- A sociotechnical imaginary is more than an individual prediction: look for projected social order, institutions, collective futures, agents of change and assumptions about governance or ownership.
- Codebooks, project background, retrieved memory, previous summaries and prior model outputs are context. They are not evidence that the current source expresses a claim.
- Abstention and explicit uncertainty are valid and preferable to unsupported theoretical classification.
